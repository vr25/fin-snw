West Midlands Police chief speaks out over ‘political and media frenzy’
The now former Chief Constable of West Midlands Police, Craig Guildford has issued a statement following his retirement from the force today
The now former chief constable of West Midlands Police has issued a statement as his retirement is confirmed.
Chief Constable Craig Guildford is stopping down from his role after a week of intensive speculation following the force’s blunders in advising the city council’s Safety Advisory Group on the controversial Aston Villa v Maccabi Tel Aviv Europa League match.
The advice led to away fans being banned, but there was condemnation of that decision and the evidence it was based on.

This week Her Majesty’s Chief Inspector of Constabulary, Sir Andy Cooke, issued a damining report on advice given to the SAG saying ‘confirmation bias’ influenced the decision.
Craig Guildford had admitted to the Home Affairs select committee on Monday (January 12) that AI (artificial intelligence) had been used in preparing a to the committee, and that wrongly identified a West Ham v Maccabi match that never took place.
He had previously denied to the committee that the force used AI.
The then chief constable said: “I became aware that the erroneous result concerning the West Ham v Maccabi Tel Aviv match arose as result of a use of Microsoft Co Pilot.
“I would like to offer my profound apology to the Committee for this error, both on behalf of myself and that of ACC O’Hara.
“I had understood and been advised that the match had been identified by way of a Google search in preparation for attending HAC.
“My belief that this was the case was honestly held and there was no intention to mislead the Committee.”
But after intensive pressure, Mr Guildford has stood down from his role, having been with the force since December 2022. He began his policing career 30 years before that in 1992.
His decision has been welcomed by the West Midlands Police and Crime Commissioner, Simon Foster and the Home Secretary, Birmingham MP Shabana Mahmood.

Confirming his retirement Mr Guildford said: “I have come to the conclusion that the political and media frenzy around myself and my position has become detrimental to all the great work undertaken by my officers and staff in serving communities across the West Midlands.
"I have carefully considered my position and concluded that retirement is in the best interests of the organisation, myself and my family.
"It has been the honour of my career serving as the Chief Constable of West Midlands Police.
"With the force’s dedicated and hard-working workforce, together we have turned this force around."

The statement continued: “I therefore want to take this opportunity to recognise the outstanding level of service that this force provides to the public.
“I know this will continue, thanks to all of the officers, staff and volunteers who work for WMP who all strive to keep the public safe.
"I would also like to thank all of the communities of the West Midlands for your support. I will greatly miss the incredible people I have had the privilege to meet, work with and serve across the region."
