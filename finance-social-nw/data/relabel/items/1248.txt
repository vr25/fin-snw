But holidays are a time for guilty pleasures so it's best to get it right. There's no point in splurging and then being disappointed. Guilt's OK but only if there's pleasure first.
Moruya Eats is a good place for a guilty splurge, and a worthy entry among our Best of the South Coast series.
"I get fresh fish from my supplier on a Friday so it's fresh for the weekend. We get fresh snapper, monkfish, swordfish, whiting, flathead. I get fresh oysters in as well over the weekend," proprietor Rachael Madsen said.
"I don't like things in a box. I buy raw products and create fresh food from the raw product.
"I do all my own crumbing my own battering. I do my own potato scallops. I do my own chicken tenders."
Homemade trumps all
Apart from fish and chips and potato scallops and calories in every other conceivable form, she bakes her own cakes, including passionfruit cheesecake at $6 a slice.
 "I'm a bit of a cake enthusiast," Rachael said.
As the line of customers lengthens, she scampers around the shop she bought on Vulcan Street three years ago to stave off what felt to her like the boredom of retirement.
She reckons the popularity of the shop stems from the freshness of the raw ingredients, but also because she doesn't skimp on the oil which makes the produce crispy.
"I change my oils regularly. That's a big trick in the industry. You've got to change your oils all the time," she said.
Many fish in the sea
Along the South Coast, there are lots of other places to indulge in deep-fried calorific naughtiness.
In Batemans Bay, a well-loved favourite of visiting Canberrans is the Innes Boatshed on Clyde Street. After all, it doesn't get fresher than a fish and chip shop next to the boats that catch the fish. 
"From the ocean to you!" the restaurant boasts. "Our fresh and cooked fish menu changes daily - depending on what we catch.
"The Innes family have been catching, cooking and serving the freshest local seafood through three generations of professional fishermen and women."
Roy's Fish & Chips Takeaway Café on Beach Road also gets a recommendation. It's not a traditional fish and chip shop in that it also specialises in Sri Lankan dishes, but both cuisines get a good rap.
The Australian Good Food Guide said that Berny's at Batehaven offers "succulent shellfish coming from Berny's own oyster leases on the Clyde River". 
"Almost everything is house-made, from hand-cut old school chips to prawn cutlets, skewers and calamari rings, while prawns are sourced directly off the trawler in Cairns, cooked on the boat and snap frozen."
Something Seafood on Emmett Street in Callalla Bay is a past winner of Best Fish & Chips in NSW and a runner-up for the best chips in Australia.
Its star turns are "signature spring rolls" and grilled fish. 
"You know that quality is not scrimped on here. Fresh, not frozen seafood is skillfully prepared, so be aware menu items can run out," the Explore South Coast website said.
