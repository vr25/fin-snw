Motorsport fans have been given a first look at the country's newest race track after a drive day at Circuit Italia to the north of Newcastle this week.

The privately-owned facility at Balickera, which was more than a decade in the making, has finally opened to the public.
Local driver Cody McKay hit the 2.9-kilometre circuit this week, one of more than 30 involved in a professional drive day held by The Drive Team.
"We've known for nearly 25 years that this track was being planned and in the making. It was definitely a pinch-ourselves moment as we were rolling through the gates in the morning," McKay, who posted a video of one of his laps on YouTube, told the Newcastle Herald.
"We thought we were in a dream. We couldn't believe it was happening.
"It's a big win for Newcastle."
Merewether-based McKay, who for years has competed in the Aussie Racing Car Championship and other events, described the circuit as a "drivers' track" and compared it to Germany's famous Nurburgring.
"It's extremely challenging," he said. "It's daunting, it's very fast, very flowing and it can be scary sometimes as well. This is definitely one of those tracks that they've put a lot of thought into, to make sure the drivers get a real thrill and experience from it.
"It's the off-camber corners and racing through the forest section ... I would probably say the closest is the Nurburgring in Germany. It seems like they've pulled a lot of inspiration from it.
"We're still trying to process it ... maybe closer towards a street circuit that runs through the forest."
Hunter businessman Matthew Higgins won approval for Circuit Italia from Port Stephens Council in 2013, but a previous proposal for a similar facility on the site dates back to the early 2000s.
Construction took several years, and there has been plenty of industry anticipation about it opening for public use, given it is the only circuit between Sydney and south-east Queensland.
"To get to this point is both personally and professionally satisfying," Circuit Italia general manager Damien White said.
"I have been involved in motorsport since I was eight years old, and have been very fortunate to have experienced some amazing things, but nothing matches the feeling of seeing and hearing the enjoyment of the people that were here [on Wednesday]."
The track is 12-metres wide and extends to 15 metres on the start and finishing straight. It will ultimately be able to accommodate racing.
Almost 12,000 tonnes of gravel has been placed in run-off areas, meeting standards of the FIA (Federation Internationale de l'Automobile).
There is still some polish to be applied to the broader facilities, but the circuit is expected to host races in coming years - and potentially Supercars, as White has previously indicated.
"It is open to the public," White said.
"Our website will be undergoing a revamp which will include a showing of available dates for drive days or private circuit hire.
"With regards to racing, the circuit has been built with the intention to satisfy the regulatory requirements for any category of motorsport other than Formula 1.
"We have had numerous racing categories approach us to open discussions on including us in their calendar.
"With our first day of operation now behind us we will start exploring all types of opportunities."
McKay, who continues in the Aussie Racing Cars in South Australia next month before making his debut in the V8 SuperUte Series at the Bathurst 1000 in October, said it was an exciting time for motorsport in the Hunter and the industry at large.
In addition to Circuit Italia launching, there is another track under construction at Wakefield near Lake Macquarie.
Approved in 2019, the Black Rock Motor Resort will feature a 5.4-kilometre long track, but it is not expected to host racing.
"That's going to be a world-class facility," McKay said. "We've gone from no asphalt race tracks for 30 or 40 years, to all of a sudden we will have two within half-an-hour of Newcastle CBD.
"We've seen what happens overseas at places like Highlands [in New Zealand], it's amazing not only the circuit but the industry that's set up right next to it in the industrial areas and all that type of stuff.
"People will want to come here and do track days and race their cars. It's great that Newcastle now has something we can offer to the world."
