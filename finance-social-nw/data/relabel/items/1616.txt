Donald Trump arrives in United Kingdom for historic second visit as US President to meet King Charles
US President Donald Trump and First Lady Melania Trump have touched down in the United Kingdom for a historic second state visit.
Mr Trump visited the United Kingdom as US President in June 2019, where he met with Queen Elizabeth II and with then-Prime Minister Theresa May.
Since his last visit just six years ago, the UK has had five prime ministers. After Ms May was ousted by her Tory colleagues, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss and Rishi Sunak have led the Conservative Party.
Then in 2024, the UK voted in Labour, led by Prime Minister Keir Starmer.
Following the Queen’s passing in 2022, King Charles became monarch, with Mr Trump set to meet the new top royal on Wednesday UK time.
“My relationship is very good with the UK, and Charles, as you know, who’s now King, is my friend,” Mr Trump told reporters ahead of landing.
“It’s the first time this has ever happened where somebody was honoured twice. So, it’s a great honour.”
He added: “I’m there also on trade. They want to see if they can refine the trade deal a little bit.
“We’ve made a deal, and it’s a great deal, and I’m into helping them.”
When Mr Trump and the First Lady arrived, they descended the stairs of Air Force One before shaking hands with awaiting dignities.
The two were then taken to Winfield House in central London on Marine One.
The Trumps were greeted at Winfield House by US Ambassador to the UK Warren Stephens and his wife.
“Our fantastic ambassador — we appreciate the job he’s doing,” Mr Trump told media.
“He’s done really well. The relationships are great and he’s been outstanding, so I want to thank him for welcoming us...
“Tomorrow’s going to be a very big day.”
Mr Trump spoke highly about his upcoming meeting with King Charles.
“We’re going to see him tomorrow and he’s been a friend of mine for a long time — and everybody respects him,” he said.
“Everybody loves him.”
Sir Keir wants to pitch Britain as a destination for US investment, closely aligning its financial services, tech and energy sectors with larger American peers to try to drive much-needed economic growth at home.
To that end, business executives including Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang and OpenAI’s Sam Altman are due to attend, while billions of dollars of business deals are expected to be announced.
Microsoft said it would invest more than $US30 billion ($A45 billion) in Britain over the next four years, while Google said it would invest STG5 billion ($A10 billion), partly on a new data centre close to London that would help meet demand for AI services.
A spokesperson for the UK Prime Minister described the state visit as “a historic opportunity” coming “at a crucial time for global stability and security”.
“The Prime Minister will discuss the challenges that both our countries face, and the opportunities, as we enter a new era of our deep and unparalleled relationship,” the spokesperson told reporters.
Sir Keir will turn the focus to foreign affairs on Thursday when he hosts Mr Trump at his Chequers country residence and will attempt to draw a line under his handling of the departures of his deputy, Angela Rayner, and Peter Mandelson.
He gave both his full-throated support before being forced to let them go, prompting questions over his political judgment at a time when Nigel Farage’s populist Reform UK party holds a hefty lead in the polls.
