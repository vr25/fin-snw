Spotify shares jump after CEO plans to lay off 17% of workforce
The music streaming service, Spotify, announced it's reducing its global workforce in a third round of layoffs this year.
Spotify plans to get rid of about 1,500 workers. This comes after the company laid off 700 workers earlier this year.
In an email sent to staff, CEO Daniel Ek blamed the "slowing economic growth" and "increased cost of capital."
"To align Spotify with our future goals and ensure we are right-sized for the challenges ahead, I have made the difficult decision to reduce our total headcount by approximately 17% across the company. I recognize this will impact a number of individuals who have made valuable contributions. To be blunt, many smart, talented and hard-working people will be departing us," Ek's memo read in part.
Workers impacted are expected to be notified by the end of Tuesday.
Ek said the "leaner structure" of the company will ensure "Spotify's continued profitability."
Spotify shares closed up more than 7% Monday after news of layoffs.
Spotify reportedly added 6 million subscribers in Q3. It's growth in the last quarter had more than 2 million subscribers than anticipated.
The company announced in January that it was axing 6% of total staff. In June, it cut staff by another 2%, or about 200 workers, mainly in its podcast division.
Tech companies like Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Meta and IBM have announced hundreds of thousands of job cuts this year.
Editor's Note: The Associated Press contributed to this report.
