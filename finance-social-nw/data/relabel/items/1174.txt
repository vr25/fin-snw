HANOI, Vietnam, U.S. technology firms, data protection rules, cross-border data transfers, social media platforms, data center operators, Facebook, data center industry, foreign investment, tech companies, social media providers, Jason Oxman, Information Technology Industry Council Meta, Google, Equinix.
Vietnam, with its 100 million residents, is a significant market for platforms like Facebook and aims to boost its data center industry through foreign investment. However, the proposed law could make it difficult for tech companies and social media providers to serve their daily users, warned Jason Oxman, chair of the Information Technology Industry Council (ITI), which represents firms like Meta, Google, and data center operator Equinix.
The legislation, currently under parliamentary review and potentially set for approval on November 30, aims to strengthen government access to data. It mandates prior authorization for transferring "core" or "important" data overseas, though these terms remain loosely defined. Vietnam's public security ministry, which proposed the law, has not commented on these concerns.
While existing regulations already limit some cross-border data flows, they are rarely enforced. The new law could add significant compliance burdens for tech companies. Adam Sitkoff, executive director of the American Chamber of Commerce in Hanoi, noted ongoing discussions to encourage authorities to reconsider the "rushed" legislative timeline, as the law could pose major compliance issues for private firms.
Vietnam's parliament's swift movement on this draft comes amid ambitions to position the nation as a regional data center hub, especially as restrictions on foreign ownership are set to lift next year. Yet, firms like Google, which previously eyed data center expansion in Vietnam, may reconsider if the law passes.
The proposed data-sharing requirements would also oblige companies to provide information to the Communist Party and state agencies for broad purposes, including vaguely defined "public interest" tasks. Oxman added that the U.S. tech industry remains concerned about the "undue expansion of government access to data," as Vietnam's new law may ultimately challenge foreign business operations and investment in its digital sector.
