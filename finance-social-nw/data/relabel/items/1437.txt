Trump prepares to lower tariffs on China
Donald Trump has signalled he could be open to a climbdown in his trade war on China, as he said an 80pc tariff on goods from the country "seems right".
The seeming about-turn on his most severe policies, which included tariffs of as much as 145pc, comes ahead of meetings this weekend in Switzerland between Scott Bessent, Mr Trump's Treasury Secretary, and Chinese leaders.
The US President posted on his Truth Social website: "80% Tariff on China seems right! Up to Scott B."
Tariffs spiralled as China retaliated to American border levies, leading to a tit-for-tat rise in the taxes charged by each of the world's two largest economies on imports of goods from the other.
China's tariffs on American goods hit 125pc, levels which economists view as an effective embargo on most trade between the two.
China's exports to the US last month fell by more than 20pc compared with April of 2024, according to official data. But overall the country's sales to foreign nations increased by 8pc, with a rise in purchases by customers in Europe and the rest of Asia more than making up for the drop in exports across the Pacific.
Neither side has been keen to be the first to back down in the trade war, with the White House previously insisting that Beijing would need to make the first move.
Xiangrong Yu, economist at Citi, said there is little chance of the trade war coming to an end any time soon.
"Given the wide gap in views between the US and China, however, it will likely be a long and bumpy process for them to reach a meaningful and lasting trade deal," he said.

Markets primed for de-escalation as Trump offers to cut tariffs on Chinese goods
Donald Trump's semaphore on Truth Social on Friday, where he suggested he was open to almost halving the punitive 145pc tariff on Chinese imports, will stoke market expectations for the crunch US-China peace talks in Geneva this weekend, reports Hans van Leeuwen.
US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Trade Representative Jamieson Greer will on Saturday meet Chinese Vice-Premier He Lifeng – the first time official meeting between the two sides since before the outbreak of the "liberation day" trade war.
Markets and businesses have been heartened by the increasingly emollient rhetoric from both sides in the lead-up to the talks, and by a subsequent Trump post that there were many more bilateral trade deals "in the hopper".
Bessent has spoken of de-escalating the trade war, and Trump told reporters in the Oval Office on Thursday that the tariff rate on China "can't get any higher" and "we know it's coming down". On Friday, he floated a figure of 80pc – which, while still painful, at least makes the trade barrier a little more surmountable.
Of course he'll want a quid pro quo. In a separate all-caps Truth Social post just minutes earlier, Trump called on China to "open up its market". "WOULD BE SO GOOD FOR THEM!!! CLOSED MARKETS DON'T WORK ANYMORE!!!" he wrote, in a seemingly remarkable conversion to the benefits of free trade.
His focus seems to be on headline tariff rates, but the talks are also expected to cover the US decision to levy tax on small parcels – something American voters will really notice – and also China's controls on rare-earth exports to the US.
Beijing's de facto rare-earths ban is probably its strongest negotiating card, to use a favourite Trumpian term. This, even more than the broader economic impact of shutting down goods trade with China, is surely what has brought Washington to the table so early and so ready to parley.
But as the US-UK trade deal showed, Trump needs to be able to hold up a flashy chart that demonstrates a win on his terms. So China will need to reciprocate Trump's gesture by also slashing its 125pc tariff on American goods. The question is what Washington can offer in return for a resumption of the rare earths trade, and whether it's a concession Trump won't mind seeing on Truth Social.
