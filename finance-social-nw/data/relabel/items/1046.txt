Under questioning from Nationals MLC Sarah Mitchell during Budget Estimates on August 28 the premier admitted he was unaware of a letter sent by Australian Community Media (owner of this publication) managing director Tony Kendall and Country Press NSW president Lucie Peart requesting the funding be delivered.
Mr Minns also said he was unaware of how many publications had shuttered in the past year in regional NSW.
It comes as storied ACM mastheads such as Moree Champion, Tenterfield Star, The Inverell Times, Glen Innes Examiner, Country Leader, Dungog Chronicle, Gloucester Advocate and Milton-Ulladulla Times will cease publishing a weekly print edition.
Ms Mitchell asked Mr Minns at Estimates on August 28: "Under your government the advertising spend is down 40 per cent in the last 12 months, that means people are losing their jobs, that means regional people who rely on those newspapers for access to information are missing out because you haven't spent the money that you said you would in terms of advertising spend, what are you going to do about it?"
The premier described the question as a "glib recitation of circumstances relating to the economic circumstances of newspapers".
Mr Minns attributed the closure of some regional newspapers to Meta, the owner of Facebook and Instagram, abandoning a deal to pay for journalism in March this year.
Meta had voluntarily entered into commercial agreements with a number of Australian media organisations, including ACM, in 2021 to publish their news coverage via a dedicated feed called Facebook News."
"There was a pool of funds that was distributed, it continues to be distributed by Google but Meta withdrew from the application...that is affecting regional media right across the country," Mr Minns said.
"That is the leading pressure, the massive pressure when it comes to regional newspapers in NSW."
The premier said he would meet with ACM and Country Press NSW but did not commit to any further funding for regional newspapers.
The Minns government promised an additional $3 million in government advertising for regional print media before the election.
The promised expenditure was scaled back to $1 million a year in September. 
NSW Government advertising spend in regional newspapers fell by more than 40% in the last 12 months.
Since then, more than a dozen publications at ACM have closed including the Lismore News and Cessnock Advertiser.
ACM is the leading publisher of regional newspapers in NSW and around Australia.
