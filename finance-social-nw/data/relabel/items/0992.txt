NEW YORK, New York - U.S. stocks continued higher on Thursday after the Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis advised Gross domestic Product (GDP) increased at a 2.8 percent annualized rate in the June quarter, well above the 2.0 percent the markets expected.
"The economy is in a transition, but it's in a good place," Ryan Sweet, chief U.S. economist at Oxford Economics told The New York Times Thursday. "The economy is slowing from very strong growth in the second half of last year. We're just settling down into something that's a little more sustainable."
However, as the day wore on sellers took charge in the tech sector in particular and this carried over to industrial bourses. Shares in Nvidia, Microsoft and Alphabet all lost ground in late trading towards the close.
"There's a changing of the guard happening on Wall Street. The AI stocks that led on the way up are now leading on the way down," Adam Sarhan, CEO of 50 Park Investments told CNBC Thursday.
"During bull markets, you see one sector lead, then it pauses, corrects and passes the baton," he said. "Think of it like a relay race over to another sector."
Ford was probably the biggest lose of the day, tumbling 18.4 percent on the back of its second quarter results which fell well short of what was expected.
