SAN FRANCISCO, California: To comply with European Union (EU) regulations, Meta Platforms is planning to introduce an ad-free subscription plan for Instagram and Facebook users in Europe.
The plan, which could potentially cost €10 ($10.49) per month, is in response to EU regulations that threaten the company's ability to personalize ads for users without their consent, negatively affecting its primary revenue source.
Meta aims to comply with regulations without affecting its ad business by offering a free, ad-supported plan or a paid subscription, which could encourage users to choose the former.
Meta will also factor in commissions charged by Apple's and Google's app stores, so the price for a single account could rise to €13 on mobile devices.
Earlier this year, Meta was fined €390 million by Ireland's Data Privacy Commissioner, which ruled that the company cannot use the so-called "contract" as a legal basis to send users ads based on their online activity.
In response, the social media company said it would ask users in the EU for their consent before allowing businesses to target ads.
A Meta spokesperson said the company believes in "free services which are supported by personalized ads," but is exploring "options to ensure we comply with evolving regulatory requirements."
