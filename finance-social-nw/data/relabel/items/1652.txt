Menai Bridge closure slammed with calls for height restriction on both sides
The Menai Suspension Bridge remains closed after engineers said bolts on beams under the bridge needed replacing
North Wales Live readers have expressed their strong opinions following the recent closure of the Menai Bridge. Some, having viewed live traffic updates, felt relieved they were not on the road.
The Menai Suspension Bridge remains shut after engineers determined that bolts on beams beneath the bridge needed replacing.
The bridge was closed at 2pm on Saturday by order of the Welsh Government, with Transport Secretary Ken Skates apologising for the inconvenience caused.
The government has indicated that enforcement of weight restrictions has been problematic, suggesting that a new traffic management plan could pave the way to reopening.
On Sunday, Mr Skates met with the leadership of UK Highways A55 - who maintain the bridge - to discuss the temporary closure and the next steps.
Here's what some of our readers had to say about the closure, Edward James writes: "Just been looking at Google maps. Glad I'm not trying to get into Bangor. This would never happen in China. They'd have a new bridge up in 6 months. But then China is run by engineers - competent people."
Puffinisland12 says: "I just worry for the couple of painters that have been working so hard with their one inch paint brush all year. I hope they will be back to keep painting and save the bridge. Once we get another 36 AMs in Cardiff, everything will be better in Wales."
Julius72 adds: "Essentially, what the Welsh Assembly Government is suggesting is that they're unable to swiftly implement a traffic management plan to allow only cars over the Menai Bridge, so they've decided to close it to everyone until they can sort things out."
Mayorofthedunghill believes: "No doubt a politician will surface in the next day or two to propose revisiting the idea of wind deflectors on the Britannia Bridge, or perhaps commission another (yes, another) feasibility study on a third lane, or even, if they're feeling particularly ambitious, suggest that a third bridge 'could be back on the agenda'. Then it's back to slumber until the next crisis arises. If only we had politicians who knew how to manage something other than expectations."
