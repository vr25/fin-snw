HIBBING — A man charged in a fatal overdose case reached a last-minute plea agreement with prosecutors Friday.
Justin Dean Stauffer, 42, of Chisholm, pleaded guilty to first-degree sale of methamphetamine and possession of a firearm by an ineligible person.

Stauffer, who has several prior drug convictions, entered the pleas at a hearing called via Zoom, just three days before he was set to face a jury trial for the death of Timothy Robert Baker, 63, in March 2023.
A count of third-degree murder will be dismissed under the agreement with the St. Louis County Attorney’s Office, but Baker’s family will still be allowed to present victim-impact statements.
Stauffer agreed to receive a guideline person term, which defense attorney Veronica Surges said is expected to be approximately 9 ½ years. Judge Rachel Sullivan ordered a standard background investigation and set sentencing for May 15.
According to court documents:
Baker's body was found in his vehicle on the 500 block of Sixth Street Northwest on March 13, 2023. Officers noted he was "laying on his side on the passenger seat of the vehicle, appearing as if someone moved him to that location." His phone was found near the body.
Police reviewed audio of a 911 call that had been placed from the phone around 7:15 p.m. on March 8. Lasting 4 1/2 minutes, it included only apparent “agonal breathing” — the body's last-ditch effort to remain alive. Officers indicated Baker "would have been essentially unconscious during this breathing, leading to the conclusion another individual made the call from the phone."
A search of Baker's phone data showed that he had been in contact with a "Francis Kane" on Facebook, asking March 2 if the other user had "any fast" — a slang term for drugs, typically meth. "Kane" responded with "just a little."

Records indicated the two next communicated on the afternoon of March 8, with Baker indicating he was in Chisholm and telling "Kane" he "should "smoke some s--- with me."
Investigators obtained a warrant for Facebook records and confirmed that the "Kane" account actually belonged to Stauffer, who was also known to go by the nickname "Shrimp."
Another warrant for Google records revealed that the two were in communication on March 8, with Baker traveling to the area of Stauffer’s residence before his phone traveled a half-mile to the location where his body was ultimately discovered.
Investigators said a review of Stauffer's phone records showed that he was "actively selling and purchasing controlled substances" around the time of Baker's death.
Meanwhile, Stauffer acknowledged in a March 12 message to the victim’s daughter that he had provided Baker drugs, but claimed someone else was in the truck with him and said he asked them to leave so as to avoid police attention.
Police executed a warrant at Stauffer's home March 21. In a bedroom, officers found a .223-caliber rifle, ammunition, more than 56 grams of suspected meth, more than 8 grams of suspected fentanyl, a small quantity of suspected cocaine and several containers of apparent prescription pills, including morphine, amphetamine and clonazepam.
Other paraphernalia, including digital scales, an apparent formula for mixing meth and fentanyl and pricing details, also were found in the bedroom. The defendant, after being arrested, was heard by officers yelling at a woman to "take the charges and tell the police that it was her."

Records indicate Stauffer has at least six prior felony convictions in Minnesota, including three for drugs. He remains at the St. Louis County Jail.
