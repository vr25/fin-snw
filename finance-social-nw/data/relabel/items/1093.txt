The Chinese central bank's largest stimulus measures since Covid should provide the world's second-largest economy with a much-needed boost, which should help our portfolio companies doing business there. The People's Bank of China overnight announced reductions in short-term interest rates and bank reserve requirements. It also unveiled a plan to boost the ailing Chinese property sector and boost liquidity in China's stock market , which led the way in Asia trading and provided support on Wall Street . Commodities also got a leg up on hopes a better economy in China would increase demand. Oil prices were up more than 1%. Copper , a global consumption proxy, rose 3% to its highest levels since July. Questions remain, however, whether the Chinese central bank's monetary actions will be enough to get China out of its economic hole without accompanying fiscal stimulus. The economy there has been growing less than the government's target rate. China's recovery following the pandemic has been slower and shallower than most industrialized nations. Another wildcard for corporate America is the upcoming U.S. presidential election. Republican nominee Donald Trump promises to be tougher on China than when he was in the White House. Democratic nominee Kamala Harris is running on the Biden administration's record on China, which has continued many of the Trump tariffs and taken actions on its own, such as export restrictions on the most powerful U.S.-designed artificial intelligence semiconductors. The Club has a broad range of companies with significant sales exposure to China, and some with none. Here's a look at the percentages in our 32 stock portfolio, as well as recent earnings call commentary, about the business climate and the potential in China. The China sales exposure for our chipmakers — Broadcom , Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices — may skew higher than in the data because sales are allocated to individual countries based on where the products are initially billed, even if the revenue is attributable to end customers in different locations. Directionally, Nvidia and AMD have seen their percent of sales from China drop since we conducted this exercise in August 2023. Nevertheless, Nvidia has seen some recent sequential growth in its data center business in China as customers in the country adopt its modified chips that comply with U.S. export restrictions. "As a percentage of total data center revenue, [China] remains below levels seen prior to the imposition of export controls. We continue to expect the China market to be very competitive going forward," CFO Colette Kress said on Nvidia's late August earnings call. Among our China related consumer stocks in portfolio, Apple and Starbucks immediately come to mind because both companies have seen weakness in sales there. Investors are hoping to see the iPhone 16 do better than its predecessor in China as local smartphone competitors, including Huawei at the high end, have muscled their way into the conversation. Apple's business in the greater China region declined 6.5% on an annual basis, though it was less than 3% on a constant currency basis. "That is an improvement from the first half of the fiscal year, and so we're happy to see the acceleration," CEO Tim Cook said on the Aug. 1 call. For Starbucks and new CEO Brian Niccol, what to do in China is an open question. The coffee company's former leadership had big plans for China expansion. Starbucks also caught a downgrade to a sell-equivalent rating at Jefferies, which pressured the stock Tuesday. Consumer products giant Procter & Gamble has also been hurt in China, particularly in the beauty category. "The market continues to be very soft. Consumer confidence is still low. Consumers are very value conscious," P & G CFO Andre Schulten said earlier this month at a Barclays conference. The executive had recently returned from a trip to China. "We see stabilization of the trend, but we don't yet see recovery." Health-care stimulus packages in China have been slow to roll out, and that's what we're waiting on for GE Healthcare , which took China out of their guidance last quarter, and Danaher as expectations for its China business have been reset. The stimulus these companies are awaiting is different than what was announced Tuesday. "We would expect to see the benefit of stimulus having an impact in 2025," GE Healthcare CEO Peter Arduini said on the company's July 31 earnings call. "And, obviously, when it does come forward, we'll take advantage of it." For their part, Danaher executives also described the benefit from health-care focused stimulus as being a 2025 event "as people are waiting for their shovel-ready projects to receive funding." On the industrial side, DuPont has the most China exposure because of its electronics and water businesses. Eaton and Dover don't have nearly as much China exposure. We view all three companies as derivative AI plays, supporting the semiconductor industry and the data center buildouts to handle accelerated computing. Fortunately for DuPont, its business started to show signs of life in China even before the central bank's support measures were announced. For example, "market conditions in water solutions also improved during the second quarter with net sales up 12% sequentially, which was ahead of our expectations and driven primarily by an initial recovery in China," CEO Lori Koch said on its most recent earnings call. Companies with little-to-zero percentage of sales from China include three of our Fed rate cut plays. Banking on lower U.S. interest rates boosting housing activity, Stanley Black & Decker , Home Depot , and Best Buy should be rather insulated from China except for how moves there might impact the global and U.S. economy. Coterra Energy conducts 100% of its oil and natural gas exploration and production in the U.S. While it does not list any exposure to China, demand from that economic powerhouse impacts global energy prices. So to that extent, the direction of commodity prices impacts Coterra's performance. — CNBC's Jeff Marks, director of portfolio analysis for the Investing Club, contributed to this report.
