With many communities across Ontario getting dumped with snow — leaving residents clearing the mess left by snowplows at the end of driveways — the debate about municipal clean-up is resurfacing.
In communities like Brampton, residents are asking for the cost of windrow cleanup. Early estimates show it would cost about $19.6 million, which comes to an average of $86 per home per year.
In Barrie, staff was also asked for a costing. A Dec. 10, 2025 staff report from the city’s director of operations said the bill would be $12 to $13 million, along with an equipment bill of $8.5 million to buy 45 front-end loaders equipped with hydraulic windrow gates. Another $250,000 would be used for electronic software and hardware. And also the salaries for 38 full-time staff members come with the cost.
But with some communities getting buried in snow this winter, the cost may outweigh the hassle.
Last winter, a video shared by a woman in Willowdale of a special snowplow clearing the end of the driveway is causing other Ontario residents to turn pea green with envy.
The video, shared by Beth Crane on Ontario Storm Reports Facebook page on Feb. 16, 2025 shows a snowplow with an extra arm that comes down to push snow across the bottom of a driveway, instead of leaving a mound of heavy snow behind. The video had 780,000 views and more than 820 comments within days.
“Truly the best equipment the city of Toronto ever purchased. Windrow plows,” Crane shared in the post. “It scoops a good 4-5 feet into the driveway instead of packing it with a solid wall.”
“All cities should have this, lots of elderly that can’t shovel that heavy stuff at end of driveways,” Kim Hawkins said on the Facebook post.
“This equipment is not used everywhere in Toronto,” Rachel Harney said in the Facebook post. “When the city amalgamated, only those areas who had it before were grandfathered in. North York was one of the lucky ones. With all the street parking, it would be impossible to use where I live in East York.”
“So the city that rarely gets snow has a special plow? Huh. The rest of Ontario has learned to accept that pile of driveway snow as the cost of having cleared roads,” Dan O’Gorman wrote on Facebook.
Toronto has 147 windrow plows
According to the City of Toronto, there are 147 windrow plows, and they are being used in Toronto, East York District, and parts of North York.
“All equipment used in the new winter contract in 2022 was brought in by the new winter contractors as they were required to select and supply the type and amount of equipment required to perform the services,” Toronto spokesperson Shane Gerard said, adding nothing was “grandfathered in” from the 1998 amalgamation. “Contract vendors were required to select the type and amount of equipment needed to provide the winter services.”
Gerard said they seem to be effective.
Other municipalities with windrow clearing
In 2023, the City of Vaughan had special windrow cleaning machines, but it would only clear a portion of the driveway entrance, and the city said it would be cleared within four hours after a snowplow has passed.
Richmond Hill and Markham voted in favour of having windrows cleared back in 2019. Back then, the cost for Richmond Hill was $4.38 million, and Markham’s tab came in at $4.4 million.
Ajax also looked into the effort in 2019, but decided against the $1.8 million cost.
Whitby estimated its cost would be $3.7 million back in 2023.
New this winter, Mississauga residents with single, shared and double driveways get at least a three-metre space cleared in their driveway windrow. No application or registration is required. The city will not clear windrows for condominiums, commercial properties and homes on private roads. The service only happens when Mississauga gets at least five centimetres of snow. Driveway windrows will be cleared four to six hours after your street has received its final round of plowing. Visit mississauga.ca/snow for details.
Milton also offers a Windrow Removal program for $69. The program runs until March 31.
Burlington offers a Windrow Clearing program for $128.75 plus tax, any time it snows more than seven-and-a-half centimetres. It runs until March 31.
Aurora ran a Windrow Clearing as a pilot project in 2025. Older adults, or those living with disabilities, had to pay $100 for the service, which ran until April 1 last year.
In Oakville, a service opens up to higher priority residents first, costing $184 plus HST. Its program runs until March 31.
In Brampton, the a survey is still online asking if residents would be interested in paying extra for windrow clearing services. Visit letsconnect.brampton.ca for details.
